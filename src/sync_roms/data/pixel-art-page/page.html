Pixel art

Every icon and picture sync-roms draws for its spruceOS and ES-DE pixel themes (not the bars, panels and boxes behind the menus), one art pixel to one image pixel. Pick a picture to see it on a pixel grid below its row, with the theme files it goes in and their sizes; the address then links straight to it.

For pixel artists

The Pixel themes for spruceOS (PyUI) and ES-DE are drawn by code today. This brief is for pixel artists who'd like to draw them by hand. It lists every picture a hand-drawn set would replace, the exact canvas each one has to fit, and the rules that keep the set consistent. The pictures above are the checklist and the reference: each one is at one art pixel per image pixel, with its theme file names.

How your pixels get to the screen

You draw at 1×: one art pixel is one PNG pixel. The build scales your art by a whole number with nearest-neighbour (no smoothing), places it on the theme's fixed-size file, and writes copies for each screen size. Each table below gives the art box, the most art pixels a picture may use, including its outline.

The build centres your art on the file's anchor point, so you don't need to align it. Use an even width when the drawing is symmetric, so its middle lands exactly on the anchor.

Rules for every picture

File format

PNG, RGBA, at 1×. Transparent background. Each pixel is either fully opaque or fully transparent: no anti-aliasing, no soft edges, no semi-transparent pixels.

Outline

A 1 px outline around every shape, in ink #1b1530. The outline counts toward the art box. Keep at least a 1 px clear border inside the box.

Light and shading

Light comes from the top left. Use 2 to 3 tones per colour. Our code lightens a shape's top edge about 38% toward white and darkens its bottom edge about 34% toward black; staying near that keeps your pictures in line with the rest.

Clean lines

Mirror symmetric hardware exactly. Straight and diagonal edges step in regular 1:1, 2:1 or 1:2 runs, curves step evenly, and outlines have no jogs or stray pixels. Crooked sprites get sent back.

Background

Everything shows on black, or on a palette's dark bar colour. Check each picture on #000000 and make sure it stands out.

No words

Don't draw text into a picture unless the brief asks for it (the hint pills). Titles, labels and menus are set in the theme font, Fusion Pixel 12 px.

Selected and unselected

Draw the selected (full colour) state only. The build makes the unselected state itself by desaturating 50% and darkening to 62%. If you want a different unselected picture, send it too as <id>-n.png.

Match the neighbours

Similar things are drawn at similar sizes: two handheld consoles should be within about 4 px of each other in each direction. Compare with the existing picture above.

Sprite colours

The colours our system, menu, app and status icons are drawn in today. These icons keep the same colours in every theme palette.

About 480 pictures, plus 20 optional regional ones. The bars, panels, list highlights and boxes behind the menus stay as they are and aren't part of this brief. The art box is the largest drawing that fits at the scale given. Our existing pictures are usually a little smaller than the box.

Palette icons

These are the only menu pictures spruceOS loads that change with the palette: the button hint pills in the bottom bar. Each theme is built in 41 palettes, so they are painted with key colours instead of real ones. The build swaps each key for that palette's colour. Preview your work with the Gruvbox column.

Accent highlight and accent shadow come from the palette's accent colour (35% toward white, 40% toward black). Ink is the same in every palette.

  • They are drawn on a grid of 2 px square cells. Draw at 1 px per cell, on the grid in the table.
  • Where the file is bigger than the grid, the leftover pixels are split evenly around your art, so you don't need to position it.
  • Each pill holds one button letter (A, B or X) in a 13×13 grid. context-a, context-b and context-x are the contextual bottom bar's; icon-A-54 and icon-B-54 are the A and B pills again, for the standard bottom bar.
  • Draw each icon once at this 640×480 grid. The theme's other screen sizes get scaled copies.

Collection box art

Each collection (a tag such as RPGs, Shmups or Cave games) gets a retro game box. You draw only the picture on it.

  • 32×32 pixels, including the outline and a clear border.
  • Outline in #181020. Colours come from the 38-colour box palette (box-palette.json in the sync-roms repository). Send a PNG in those exact colours, or the same grid as a text file with one palette letter per pixel.
  • The picture must stand out against its box colour, which depends on the collection.
  • The box is 84×120 and is scaled ×4 to 336×480. The picture sits on it at ×2, so each of your pixels is 8×8 screen pixels on the largest box. The 210×300 and 140×200 copies are smooth downscales, so keep details at least 2 pixels wide to survive them.
  • The title, badge and box are drawn around the picture. Don't draw text.

Where the system icons show

spruceOS

The PNG is shown as it is at 640×480. On other screens the build makes copies scaled by 1.1×, 1.16×, 1.5× or 1.6× with nearest-neighbour, so on those screens some art pixels come out one screen pixel wider than others. Avoid 1-pixel details that carry meaning, such as a 1-pixel slot that has to read as a slot.

ES-DE

The same 120×130 PNG. The selected system is drawn at 2× or 3× depending on the screen, so each art pixel is 8 or 12 screen pixels. The ones beside it are shown at half that size. All 101 ES-DE systems need a console and a controller.

Drawing a system

  1. Find photos of the real console (front and three-quarter views) and its standard controller. Note the body outline, the disc or cartridge slot, logo stripe, and the button layout and colours. Tell us which reference you used.
  2. Draw the console the way it looks in the games and the box art, not a generic lookalike.
  3. Check it at 1× and at 4× on black, next to two or three existing icons of similar hardware.
  4. Then draw its controller in the same colours and at a similar size.
